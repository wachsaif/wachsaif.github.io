Why Is My Website Not Showing Up on Google?

You built the thing, you hit publish, and Google is acting like it doesn't exist. Here's why that happens, and exactly how to fix it, from someone who debugs this for a living.

Wach Saif SEO & Technical SEO

You typed your own business name into Google. Nothing. Not even a listing on page five. Maybe you tried your main keyword next, just to make sure you weren't losing your mind. Still nothing.

I get some version of this message almost every week, usually from a small business owner who just paid someone to build a nice new website and now feels like they got scammed. They didn't. A website not showing up on Google is one of the most common problems in SEO, and in most cases it costs nothing to fix.

Below is the exact process I run through with clients: why this happens, how to check what's actually going on behind the scenes, and the order to fix things in, whether your site launched yesterday or has been sitting invisible for six months.

First, check the basics

Before you touch any technical settings, rule out the two boring, obvious causes first. Honestly, about half the messages I get about a website not showing up on Google boil down to one of these two things, and neither takes more than five minutes to check.

Is your site actually indexed?

"Not showing up" can mean two completely different things, and people mix them up constantly. Either your site is ranking poorly for a specific search, or Google hasn't indexed it at all, meaning it's not in the database in any form. Those two problems need different fixes, so figure out which one you actually have before doing anything else. It takes about two minutes, and I'll show you how in the next section.

Did you just launch your website?

If your site went live a few days ago, take a breath, because this might not be a problem at all. It might just be timing. Google has no idea your website exists until one of its crawlers actually visits it, and that doesn't happen the second you hit publish. A brand new site with no sitemap and no backlinks can sit completely un-crawled for weeks, just waiting. The fix isn't complicated. You basically just have to tell Google you exist, which is covered a bit further down.

Common reasons your website isn't showing up on Google

If your site's been live for a while and it's still a ghost on Google, one of these seven things is almost always the culprit.

1

A robots.txt file is blocking search engines

Your robots.txt file is basically a set of instructions telling search engine crawlers where they're allowed to go. The trouble is that a lot of website builders and developers leave a "Disallow: /" rule sitting in there from the staging or testing phase, and that one line blocks Google from crawling your entire site, even months after it's live to the public. I've fixed sites where this was the only issue. Everything else was perfect. One forgotten line in a text file was the whole problem.

2

A "noindex" tag got left on by accident

This one's a close cousin of the robots.txt problem. A lot of platforms automatically slap a noindex tag on your site while it's under construction, so half-finished pages don't show up in search before you're ready. Makes sense. The catch is that setting has to be switched off manually after launch, and it's easy to forget. When it stays on, Google crawls your page just fine, sees the noindex instruction, and politely leaves it out of the results anyway.

3

You never submitted a sitemap

Think of an XML sitemap as a map you hand Google, listing every page you want it to know about. Skip that step and Google has to stumble onto your pages by itself, usually by following links from other sites, which is a slow and unreliable way to find a website that barely has any links yet.

4

Nothing on the internet links to you yet

Google's crawlers find most new websites by following links from pages they already know about, kind of like following breadcrumbs. If literally no other site links to yours, and you haven't manually submitted your URL either, Google genuinely might not know you exist. Doesn't matter how good the content is. It can't rank a page it hasn't found.

5

The content is thin, or it's copied from somewhere else

If a page barely says anything, or the text is lifted from elsewhere (this includes manufacturer product descriptions and unedited template copy, by the way), Google will often crawl it and then just not bother indexing it. From its perspective, why show searchers a page that offers nothing they can't already find somewhere else?

6

Your site is slow, or it's a mess on mobile

This one's a bit different from the rest, it's a ranking problem more than an indexing one. Google factors in Core Web Vitals, mobile-friendliness, and load speed when it decides where to rank you. So your site can be fully indexed and still buried on page four, simply because it takes six seconds to load or breaks on someone's phone.

7

Your old staging site is still lurking around somewhere

A lot of developers, myself included, build sites on a temporary staging URL before moving everything to the final domain. Staging environments are deliberately password protected or hidden from search engines. Sometimes that lock accidentally carries over to the live site, or the old staging URL just stays online, duplicating your real content and quietly competing with it. Either way, Google can't index a door it isn't allowed to walk through, no matter how good the design behind it is.

One more, rarer one: a manual penalty or algorithmic filter, usually triggered by spammy backlinks, hidden text, or baggage from a previous owner if you bought a used or expired domain. Worth ruling out, and it's free to check inside Google Search Console under "Security & Manual Actions."

How to check if Google has indexed your site

The quick way: the site: search

Type site:yourdomain.com straight into Google's search bar and hit enter. See pages from your site pop up? Good, at least part of it is indexed. See "no results found" instead? Then Google hasn't indexed a single page, and you're dealing with a full indexing problem, not a ranking one.

The reliable way: Google Search Console

This is what I actually trust. Head into Google Search Console, open the URL Inspection tool, and paste in your homepage URL. It'll tell you flat out whether the page is indexed, and if it isn't, exactly why, whether that's a crawl issue, a noindex tag, or Google simply hasn't found it yet.

Step-by-step fix: getting your website indexed

Here's the order I actually work through this in. Most steps take a few minutes, and every single one of them is free.

Step 1: Verify your site in Search Console

If you haven't done this yet, go verify your website in Google Search Console now. It's free, it takes a few minutes, and it's the single most useful tool for diagnosing indexing problems. I use it on every SEO project I take on, no exceptions.

Step 2: Submit your sitemap

Inside Search Console, find Sitemaps in the sidebar and submit your sitemap.xml URL (something like yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml). This hands Google a complete list of your pages upfront, instead of hoping it stumbles onto them eventually.

Step 3: Ask Google to crawl it now

Pop your homepage into the URL Inspection tool (and any other pages that matter), then hit "Request Indexing." Think of it as a nudge, it moves you closer to the front of the line instead of waiting for Google to get to you on its own schedule.

Step 4: Clean up the technical stuff

Go check yourdomain.com/robots.txt and confirm it isn't blocking the whole site. Then peek at your homepage's source code for a stray noindex tag. Not comfortable poking around in code? Fair enough, that's exactly the kind of thing I handle for clients directly.

Step 5: Earn a handful of backlinks

Once you've confirmed you're indexed, shift focus to getting a few solid backlinks. This signals to Google that your site is worth revisiting and re-crawling on a regular basis.

Where to actually get these early on

Start with the stuff almost any business already has access to: your Google Business Profile, your social bios (Behance, LinkedIn, Facebook), local directories, and guest posts on blogs in your space. Five real, relevant backlinks will do more for you than a hundred spammy ones, and none of them need to come from some massive authority site. A handful of genuine links from real profiles is usually plenty to get a small business site noticed and crawled regularly.

Quick diagnostic checklist
  • Searched site:yourdomain.com in Google to confirm indexing status
  • Checked yourdomain.com/robots.txt for a "Disallow: /" rule
  • Checked the homepage source code for a stray noindex tag
  • Verified the site in Google Search Console
  • Submitted an XML sitemap
  • Requested indexing for the homepage

How long does it actually take?

For a technically healthy site, Google usually crawls and indexes it within a few days to two weeks of submitting a sitemap and requesting indexing. Ranking on page one for a competitive term is a whole different timeline though, realistically 3 to 6 months of steady content, on-page work, and backlinks, longer still if you're in a crowded industry.

A few things speed this up or drag it out: how often other sites in your niche get crawled (which tells Google how frequently to check back on the neighborhood), how many pages your site has, how clean your internal linking is, and how fast you fix the technical stuff once you spot it. In my experience, sites that submit a sitemap and request indexing on day one almost always get crawled faster than the ones that just wait around hoping.

Worth remembering

Indexing and ranking are not the same thing, and mixing them up causes a lot of unnecessary panic. Indexing just means Google knows your page exists and added it to its database. Ranking is where that page actually lands for a given search. You can be fully indexed and still sitting on page five, which is a ranking problem, not the one this guide is mainly about.

When to just hire someone instead of DIY-ing it

Everything above covers most indexing problems, and most business owners can work through it in under an hour. That said, it's probably time to bring in a technical SEO consultant when:

  • You've done every step above and you're still invisible after 3 to 4 weeks
  • Search Console is throwing crawl errors or a manual action you don't understand
  • You bought a used or expired domain that might be carrying baggage from its past life
  • You'd rather hand this off entirely than keep checking it yourself every month
